Lettermint is the default source. When you create an inbound route with `source: :lettermint` via `InboundRoutes.create_route/1`, the function provisions a remote Lettermint route, stores the returned `lettermint_route_id` on the local record, and generates a callback URL with `source=lettermint` for logging convenience.

The flow: Lettermint receives email at a per-organization mailbox address, then POSTs an enveloped event to Custyard's `/api/webhook/route/:callback_token` endpoint. The callback token selects the route and its `:lettermint` adapter. The adapter verifies the timestamped `X-Lettermint-Signature` over the exact raw body, then normalizes the envelope: top-level `id` (the webhook delivery id), `event`, `timestamp` and `data`. For `message.inbound`, `data` carries `from` (`email` + `name`), `to`/`cc`, `recipient` (the SMTP envelope recipient used for routing), `subaddress`, `reply_to`, `subject`, `body.text`/`body.html`, a `headers` list of `{name, value}` pairs (Message-ID, In-Reply-To, References and non-standard headers), `authentication_results`, `is_spam` and `spam_score`. The adapter produces `Name <email>`, uses `recipient` as the `to` address, prefers the text body over stripped HTML, sanitises `body.html` with an allowlist (`Custyard.Email.HtmlSanitizer`) into `metadata.html_body`, converts the header list to a map for threading lookups, and keeps only a compact metadata map (delivery id, Lettermint message id, recipient, subaddress, reply-to, CC addresses, DKIM/SPF/DMARC results, spam flag and score, the sanitised HTML, and the attachment descriptors with their signed URLs for the duration of the dispatch). Raw-message URLs, raw HTML, attachment content, `spam_symbols`, `envelope.remote_ip` and the full header list are never stored; the signed attachment URLs are consumed in memory by the attachment fetcher and never written anywhere.

Lettermint reuses the delivery `id` when it retries a webhook. The id is stored as `provider_delivery_id` on the inbound message under a unique index, and the sender-matching step returns the existing conversation for a repeated delivery without inserting a second message, even when the mail has no Message-ID header. Any other `event` (for example `message.delivered`) posted to the inbound endpoint is acknowledged with `200 {"status": "ignored", "event": ...}` so Lettermint does not retry it. The flat legacy payload (`from`, `to`, `subject`, `text`, `html`, `headers` map) is still accepted for dev fixtures.

Once a route carries inbound channels (`Custyard.InboundChannels`, see `docs/design/spec-inbound-channels.md`), the dispatcher routes each delivery by `data.recipient` alone: the local part's `+token` subaddress is matched against the channel tokens on that route, and `To`/`Cc` are never consulted. A route with no channels is a legacy route and behaves exactly as before. A recipient that matches no channel, or a channel that is paused or revoked, is written to the route's rejection log (sender, recipient, subject, classification, delivery id; swept after 14 days) and acknowledged with `200 {"status": "ignored", "reason": "no_channel" | "channel_paused" | "channel_revoked"}` so Lettermint does not retry. The adapter also surfaces `envelope.mail_from` as `metadata.mail_from`, preserving `""` because an empty envelope sender marks a bounce; `envelope.remote_ip` is still dropped.

A route can also receive mail on one custom domain for direct-MX channels. `InboundRoutes.set_custom_domain/2` sends `PUT /routes/{id}` with `{"inbound_settings": {"inbound_domain": "help.example.com"}}`, and `verify_custom_domain/1` calls `POST /routes/{id}/verify-inbound-domain`, which reports `verified`, `expected_mx_records` and `found_mx_records`. The operator publishes `MX 10 lettermint.mx` for the domain. The custom domain is never used for forwarding ingress addresses, which stay on `<route-id>.lettermint.app`. When no forwarding token matches, a recipient on the custom domain is matched, without its `+tag`, against the public addresses of the route's direct-MX channels. Sending domains are listed with `GET /domains?page[size]=100&page[cursor]=...`.

A triage stage (`Custyard.Webhooks.Triage`) then classifies the delivery before sender matching, in this order. An **own outbound echo** (the Message-ID belongs to an operator reply Custyard sent in that organization, typically the archive copy forwarded back by the operator's mailbox) is recorded as an `inbound_triaged` audit event and ignored; nothing is inserted and nobody is notified. A **provider verification** (Gmail's `forwarding-noreply@google.com` or Proton's forwarding notification, configurable via `:forwarding_verification_senders`) has its confirmation code (Gmail subject `(#digits)`) and provider link extracted; on a channel in `pending_verification` it is stored on the channel with a 24-hour TTL and acknowledged as `verification_captured`, otherwise it is logged as a `provider_notice`. It never becomes a conversation. An **auto-response** (`Auto-Submitted` other than `no`, `Precedence: bulk|junk|auto_reply|list`, `X-Autoreply`, `X-Autorespond`, or an empty envelope sender; `X-Auto-Response-Suppress` is deliberately not a signal, since it asks the recipient not to auto-reply rather than marking the message as automatic) that threads to an existing conversation is appended as a `triage_class: :auto_response` message without reactivating the conversation, without notification or enrichment and without touching the contact; one that threads to nothing is logged as `auto_response` and ignored. **Suspect** mail (`is_spam`, or DKIM and DMARC both failing) from a sender who is not a known contact in the organization still creates a conversation (open intake), flagged `suspect: true` with urgency pinned to `:normal` (a mapped priority header cannot raise it), and the notification purpose and neglect alerts are skipped until an operator clears the flag. A contact whose only conversations are themselves suspect does not count as known, so the contact row the first suspect delivery creates does not whitelist the sender; clearing the flag on any of its conversations does. Everything else is **customer** mail on the existing sender-matching path. Every stored inbound message records `inbound_channel_id`, `triage_class`, `to_addresses` (the header To addresses; the envelope recipient, which on a channel is the secret ingress address, is used for routing only and never stored on the message), `cc_addresses`, `reply_to`, the compact `authentication` summary and `spam_score`; a successful customer, suspect or auto-response delivery stamps `last_received_at` on the channel and moves a pending or awaiting channel to `active`. All ignored outcomes answer `200 {"status": "ignored", "reason": ...}`; only `{:error, _}` from the pipeline yields a 422.

Routes are created with `attachment_delivery: "url"`, so `data.attachments[]` carries `filename`, `content_type`, `size`, `content_id`, `url` and `expires_at` (signed URLs stay valid 28 days) rather than inline Base64. After the message is stored, `Custyard.Webhooks.Purposes.Attachments` fetches each attachment within the channel's `attachment_limit_mb` (default 10 MB per message) into `ATTACHMENT_DIR` under a content-addressed path, sniffs the type from the bytes and records a `message_attachments` row; attachments over the cap, on suspect mail, or on a channel with the cap at 0 are listed as skipped. Operators download through `GET /operator/attachments/:id`, which checks organization access and always serves a download. Two further triage classes sit between own echo and provider verification: **operator-sent** mail (an operator account of the organization, or the channel's public address, with an aligned DKIM pass) that threads to a conversation is appended as an operator message without delivery tracking or notification; an **operator forward** (same identity check, a `Fwd:` subject or a forwarded-message block, no thread) is unwrapped and processed as customer mail from the original sender, stamped with who forwarded it. When a channel has `archive_copy` on, every reply is BCC'd to the public address; the copy comes back through the forwarding rule and is absorbed as an own echo.

Operator replies on a channel conversation are sent from the channel's public address (or its ingress address when the channel is configured to fall back to it) with the channel display name, ahead of the route `from_address` and the operator address; `Conversations.reply_identity/2` reports which identity applies. When the last customer message carries a valid `Reply-To` that differs from the contact address, the reply is delivered to the `Reply-To` instead; auto-response and suspect messages never redirect a reply. The composer pre-fills a CC list from the latest customer message's To and CC participants minus the conversation's own addresses (reply-all), editable before sending; the list goes out as the CC header.

Other webhook sources create local routes without a Lettermint mailbox. `callback_url/1` uses each route's stored source; the query parameter is informational because the callback token controls adapter selection.

If an organization has several general routes, `get_general_route/1` prefers Lettermint, then direct email, then another source; ties use the oldest route ID. LMTP and IMAP request `source: :email` from `find_or_create_general_route/2`, which reuses a Lettermint route when one exists and otherwise selects or creates a direct email route. This keeps webhook-only Zendesk routes out of the email ingestion path.
